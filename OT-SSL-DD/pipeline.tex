\section{Method}
\label{sec:vt_method}

Given an unlabeled dataset $\mathcal{X}$, we optimize a small synthetic set
$\mathcal{S}=\{s_j\}_{j=1}^{M}$ using a frozen, self-supervised teacher $E$.
The synthetic images serve as learnable prototypes: they should organize real
images across augmentations and reproduce their feature changes between views.
Figure~\ref{fig:vt_pipeline} summarizes the pipeline.

We initialize the prototypes with representative real images. A separate,
randomly initialized encoder extracts features from the training set; K-means
groups these features into $M$ clusters, and the real image nearest each center
initializes one prototype. This encoder is then discarded. The pretrained
teacher supplies all subsequent distillation features, without updating its
weights or using class labels.

Each iteration samples a real minibatch $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{B}$ and two
differentiable augmentation draws $t_1,t_2$. Each draw is shared between the real
and synthetic sets. Writing $f(x)=E(x)/\|E(x)\|_2$, the resulting features are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 u_i=f(t_1(x_i)), &\qquad v_i=f(t_2(x_i)),\\
 a_j=f(t_1(s_j)), &\qquad b_j=f(t_2(s_j)).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:vt_features}
\end{equation}
Thus, $(u_i,v_i)$ is a real view pair and $(a_j,b_j)$ is a synthetic view pair.
Each real feature is assigned softly to the synthetic prototypes in its view:
\begin{equation}
 p^1_{ij}=\operatorname{softmax}_{j}(u_i^\top a_j/\tau),\qquad
 p^2_{ij}=\operatorname{softmax}_{j}(v_i^\top b_j/\tau).
\label{eq:vt_assignments}
\end{equation}
Here $\tau$ controls assignment sharpness. To make these assignments informative
across views, we maximize the mutual information of their joint distribution:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 P&=\operatorname{sym}\!\left((p^1)^\top p^2/B\right),\\
 I(P)&=\sum_{j,k}P_{jk}\log\frac{P_{jk}}{P_{j\cdot}P_{\cdot k}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:vt_information}
\end{equation}
We use $\operatorname{sym}(A)=(A+A^\top)/2$; $P_{j\cdot}$ and $P_{\cdot k}$
are its row and column marginals. This objective favors assignments that are
predictive across views without assigning every real image to one prototype.

We also require each synthetic pair to change like the real pairs it represents.
The average assignment $r_{ij}=(p^1_{ij}+p^2_{ij})/2$ measures the responsibility
of prototype $j$ for real pair $i$. Normalizing across real pairs gives
$w_{ij}=r_{ij}/(\sum_{\ell}r_{\ell j}+\epsilon)$.
Each pair is summarized by two quantities: its displacement and its cosine
similarity. For a synthetic pair and its weighted real targets, these are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \delta_j &= a_j-b_j,
 &\bar\delta_j &= \sum_i w_{ij}(u_i-v_i),\\
 c_j &= a_j^\top b_j,
 &\bar c_j &= \sum_i w_{ij}(u_i^\top v_i).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:vt_targets}
\end{equation}
Displacement captures the direction and magnitude of feature movement, while
cosine similarity measures how strongly the views still agree. We match them:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{move}}
 &=\frac{1}{Md}\sum_j\|\delta_j-\bar\delta_j\|_2^2,\\
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{agree}}
 &=\frac{1}{M}\sum_j\rho(c_j-\bar c_j),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:vt_matching}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the feature dimension and $\rho$ is smooth-$\ell_1$ with unit
threshold. The complete objective, with our experimental weights, is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathcal{L}=-I(P)+2\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{move}}
                         +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{agree}}.}
\label{eq:vt_objective}
\end{equation}
Real features are fixed during backpropagation, but the responsibilities
$w_{ij}$ remain differentiable through the synthetic assignments. We average
this objective over independent augmentation-pair draws and take one Adam step
on the synthetic pixels, backpropagating through the frozen teacher and the
augmentations. We clip the images to the valid range and repeat with a new
minibatch and new transformations. No network weights are optimized.

For the evaluated CIFAR-10 setting, $M=100$, $B=512$, and $\tau=0.2$.
We average two augmentation-pair draws per iteration and optimize for
$8{,}000$ iterations with a constant Adam learning rate of $10^{-3}$ on
dataset-normalized pixels. The teacher is a full-data SimCLR ResNet-18 with
BatchNorm. DSA samples one operation group per view from color, crop, cutout,
flip, scale, and rotation. After distillation, the saved images train a fresh
SSL encoder; the teacher is not reused as the evaluation encoder.
